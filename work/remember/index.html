Situation

Remember is Korea's largest professional network: 4.5M users, a quarter of the country's business professionals

The company was shifting from a utility (business card scanner) to a daily platform. Community was the key, but the MVP wasn't gaining traction. I was the sole designer, shipping almost every week.

Using Data to Find the Real Bottleneck

Problem

A PM asked me to fix the share button, I traced the full sharing flow from both sides instead

  • 0.5% share rate - Almost nobody shared posts
  • 95% drop-off - Recipients hit a login wall and left
  • Dead-end funnel - Even past the login wall, users couldn't reach posts outside their job category
Impact

New design allowed users to share posts more easily and to engage more

Approach

Pushed to ship two changes together. Easier sharing into a dead end makes it worse.

  • Floating engagement panel - Share button always visible mid-scroll, because users decided to share after reading a great comment, not at the top
  • Removed the login wall - Moved signup prompts to natural interaction moments (liking, commenting) instead of blocking access

Designing for Lasting Engagement

Problem

Gift card campaigns drove more posts. Numbers went up. But the professional tone was getting diluted.

  • Quantity over quality - Campaigns attracted low-effort posts, collect the reward, disappear
  • Core contributors ignored - The most active contributors had no recognition
  • Wrong motivation - Heavy contributors said "I want to make a positive influence." Nobody mentioned gift cards
Approach

Brought this back as a strategy question: we're incentivizing the wrong behavior.

  • Badge system - Tiered recognition showing cumulative impact on profiles, replacing one-time payouts
  • Strategy shift - Moved the team from incentive-driven to recognition-driven growth
