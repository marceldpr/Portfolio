TruScope · 2024–2025

Bringing AI into a complex
construction product.

Bringing AR, drawing management, and AI together in one construction product. A redesigned experience that improved adoption and helped teams turn complex project documents into tasks and inspections.

I started by separating visible UI problems from deeper adoption friction. As the product changed, I helped turn a business pivot into a coherent product direction.

Role
Staff product designer
Timeframe
2024–2025
Platforms
iOS, iPadOS, and web
Company
TidalHaus

The problem.

TruScope started as an engineer-built AR tool for construction teams. The core technology worked, but adoption exposed friction across onboarding, navigation, and everyday field workflows.

The insight.

Midway through the redesign, the business expanded from AR into sheet management and inspections. The product needed to support a broader construction workflow across iPhone, iPad, and web.

The solution.

I designed shared structures, navigation, and product relationships across platforms, while preserving the interaction patterns that made sense for each context.

From augmented reality
to sheet management.

TruScope was an AR app for construction work. It enabled field teams to visualize BIM models overlayed with the construction site to detect issues and gaps.

In the field.

The original AR experience focused on visualizing models and working in the field.

Across the project.

Sheet management was organized around drawings, annotations, documents, and administrative workflows. The final product had to feel like one application without forcing their fundamentally different workflows into the same visual and interaction patterns.

Two products.
One TruScope.

A connected experience across both products, with shared foundations and interactions tailored to each workflow.

01 / Connect

Shared navigation.

Connecting the products and making it clear how users move between them.

  • One connected experience across AR and sheet management.
  • Shared structures across iPhone, iPad, and web.
  • A clear path between product workflows.
02 / Organize

Consistent structure.

Organizing features and workflows around familiar patterns across both experiences.

  • Issues, inspections, tasks, and locations brought together.
  • Project information connected to its context.
  • Ownership and priorities visible at a glance.
03 / Preserve

Familiar interactions.

Preserving the interaction patterns each product’s users already know.

  • Field interactions tailored to mobile use.
  • Administrative workflows designed for the web.
  • A familiar experience with a shared product identity.
01 — 03

From navigation
to prioritization.

The previous homepage acted primarily as a menu, even though field work revolves around tasks, issues, inspections, and deadlines happening at the same time.

I redesigned it around what needs the user’s attention now. The new For You experience surfaces upcoming deadlines, assigned work, and relevant issues first, turning the homepage into a personalized starting point for the day.

  1. 01

    A homepage built around the user

    Instead of starting with product navigation, users get an immediate snapshot of the work that needs their attention.

  2. 02

    Deadlines surfaced before they become problems

    Time-sensitive issues, inspections, and tasks are prioritized so users can act without searching across the product.

  3. 03

    One place for work across the project

    Issues, inspections, tasks, locations, priorities, and linked drawings appear together instead of living in separate workflows.

  4. 04

    Navigation is still there. It just gets out of the way.

    Core areas remain one tap away, while the homepage is reserved for the information users need to make decisions.

Markups for any
type of job.

Instead of treating drawings as something teams simply annotate, I designed markups as shared points of context, connecting what happens in the field to the work managed on web.

Tasks

Turn a location on the drawing into assigned work, with ownership, priority, status, and due dates.

Links

Connect related drawings and project information at the point where teams need them.

Media

Attach visual context to a location on the drawing.

RFI

Connect a request for information to its location on the drawing.
