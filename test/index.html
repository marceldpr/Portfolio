TL/DR

Get the highlights

TruScope started as an engineer-built AR tool for construction teams.

The core technology worked, but adoption exposed friction across onboarding, navigation, and everyday field workflows.

Midway through the redesign, the business expanded from AR into sheet management and inspections.

What had been a single-purpose mobile tool now needed to support a much broader construction workflow across iPhone, iPad, and web.

The web app became as important as the field experience.

I designed shared structures, navigation, and product relationships across platforms, while preserving the interaction patterns that made sense for each context.

I introduced AI into the most repetitive parts of the workflow.

Teams could upload drawing sets and have TruScope extract, organize, and review sheet information automatically. Project manuals could also be turned into structured tasks for review and assignment.

The new experience made TruScope faster to understand, easier to use, and more valuable beyond AR.

Trial sign-ups increased 58%, core drop-off rate decreased 92%, and the final usability round reached 100% task completion.

The problem

From augmented reality to sheet management

TruScope was an AR app for construction work. It enabled field teams to visualize BIM models overlayed with the construction site to detect issues and gaps. However, they decided to enter the construction sheet-management market.

The original AR experience focused on visualizing models and working in the field. Sheet management was organized around drawings, annotations, documents, and administrative workflows. Leadership wanted the new experience to feel familiar to users of established competitors while maintaining TruScope's identity. The final product had to feel like one application without forcing their fundamentally different workflows into the same visual and interaction patterns.

Solution

Two products. One TruScope

A connected experience across both products, with shared foundations and interactions tailored to each workflow.

I shaped the experience around three design decisions, connecting the products and make it clear how users move between them, organizing features and workflows around familiar patterns across both experiences and preserving the interaction patterns each product’s users already know.

Solution

From navigation to prioritization.

The previous homepage acted primarily as a menu, even though field work revolves around tasks, issues, inspections, and deadlines happening at the same time.

I redesigned it around what needs the user’s attention now. The new For You experience surfaces upcoming deadlines, assigned work, and relevant issues first, turning the homepage into a personalized starting point for the day.
